# Loop Patterns

Loop patterns describe the recurring ways SWARM loops behave during exploration.

A loop is a single cycle of the SWARM process:

Spot → capture observations  
Weigh → evaluate importance  
Arrange → structure observations  
Refine → stress test direction  
Make → produce artifacts that create new observations

Not all loops behave the same.

Some loops generate strong observations and signals.  
Others stall, drift, or prematurely converge.

Loop patterns help practitioners recognize the state of exploration and adjust accordingly.

---

# Productive Loop

A productive loop generates new observations and strengthens signals.

Indicators:

- new observations appear during Spot
- weighing produces meaningful distinctions
- arrangement reveals new structure
- refinement exposes useful tensions
- artifacts produce additional observations

Productive loops tend to generate momentum and clarity over time.

Signals often strengthen across repeated productive loops.

---

# Shallow Loop

A shallow loop repeats activity without producing deeper observations.

Indicators:

- observations remain surface level
- weighing produces little differentiation
- arrangement does not reveal new structure
- refinement produces minor adjustments
- artifacts add little insight

Shallow loops often occur when exploration remains too safe or familiar.

Breaking a shallow loop may require expanding the observation space or producing a more provocative artifact.

---

# Stalled Loop

A stalled loop produces few or no new observations.

Indicators:

- Spot yields minimal input
- Weigh cannot meaningfully differentiate observations
- Arrange produces no new structure
- Refine produces no tension
- Make produces weak artifacts

Stalled loops often indicate:

- observation space exhaustion
- insufficient artifacts
- overly narrow exploration

Breaking a stalled loop usually requires introducing new material into the observation space.

---

# Drift Loop

A drift loop gradually moves away from the original observation cluster.

Indicators:

- new observations have weak connection to the original signals
- exploration shifts into adjacent or unrelated areas
- artifacts no longer reinforce the original direction

Drift is not always negative. It can reveal adjacent signal spaces.

However, persistent drift may indicate that the original signals were weak or misinterpreted.

---

# Premature Convergence

A direction is chosen before signals have strengthened sufficiently.

Indicators:

- decisions are made based on a small number of observations
- refinement is skipped or shortened
- artifacts aim to confirm rather than explore
- alternative paths are abandoned early

Premature convergence can produce fragile outcomes.

Healthy loops allow signals to strengthen before committing to direction.

---

# Expansion Loop

Exploration continues expanding instead of narrowing.

Indicators:

- new observations constantly expand the space
- arrangement produces larger clusters rather than clearer structure
- refinement fails to eliminate directions
- artifacts generate more possibilities instead of constraints

Expansion loops are common early in exploration.

Over time, healthy loops should begin narrowing through signal strength and refinement.

---

# Artifact Loop

Artifacts generate significant new observations.

Indicators:

- creating something immediately reveals new insights
- artifacts expose hidden assumptions
- artifacts produce unexpected feedback
- observations increase after making work

Artifact loops are often where signal strength increases rapidly.

SWARM emphasizes artifact creation because artifacts create new observation surfaces.

---

# Loop Momentum

Loops tend to develop momentum.

Productive loops often lead to stronger signals and clearer direction.

Shallow or stalled loops may persist unless exploration conditions change.

Recognizing loop patterns helps practitioners decide when to:

- expand exploration
- introduce new observations
- produce stronger artifacts
- initiate a decision checkpoint

Loop patterns describe the behavior of exploration, not the meaning of its results.
