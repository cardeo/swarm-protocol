# Judgment Patterns

Judgment patterns describe the recurring ways human judgment appears during SWARM exploration.

Signals may emerge through repeated loops, but signals alone do not determine direction.

Human practitioners interpret signals, evaluate their importance, and decide when to act.

Judgment patterns help practitioners recognize how decisions begin to form during exploration.

These patterns are not rules for decision-making. They are recognizable behaviors that appear when practitioners engage with signals over time.

---

# Intuition — Pre-Verbal Recognition

The practitioner senses that an observation matters before being able to fully explain why.

Indicators:

- a strong sense that an observation deserves attention
- the practitioner repeatedly returns to the observation
- explanation follows recognition rather than preceding it

Intuition often appears early in exploration and may guide attention toward emerging signals.

However, intuition alone does not confirm a signal. It must be validated through repeated observations across loops.

---

# Taste — Direction Filtering

The practitioner rejects directions that technically work but feel incorrect.

Indicators:

- certain options are eliminated despite appearing viable
- refinement repeatedly removes directions that lack quality
- the practitioner gravitates toward more coherent or elegant outcomes

Taste acts as a filtering mechanism during refinement.

It helps prevent weak, derivative, or incoherent outcomes from surviving the exploration process.

---

# Pattern Recognition

The practitioner recognizes structural similarities between current observations and past experiences.

Indicators:

- familiar patterns appear in new contexts
- previously encountered structures reappear
- the practitioner anticipates possible directions based on experience

Pattern recognition accelerates interpretation but does not replace exploration.

Signals should still strengthen across loops before committing to direction.

---

# False Clarity Detection

The practitioner senses that a direction appears clear but may not be sufficiently supported by signals.

Indicators:

- the explanation feels stronger than the observations supporting it
- refinement exposes contradictions
- artifacts fail to reinforce the direction

Recognizing false clarity helps prevent premature convergence.

Healthy SWARM exploration allows signals to strengthen before committing to conclusions.

---

# Signal Confidence

The practitioner begins trusting a direction as signals strengthen.

Indicators:

- multiple loops reinforce the same signal
- observations consistently support the same structure
- artifacts produce predictable outcomes

Signal confidence often leads to decision checkpoints or artifact commitments.

Confidence emerges gradually rather than appearing suddenly.

---

# Direction Lock

The practitioner commits to a direction.

Indicators:

- exploration begins focusing around a clear signal cluster
- refinement shifts toward strengthening a direction rather than exploring alternatives
- artifacts begin expressing the chosen direction

Direction lock marks the transition from exploration toward execution.

SWARM treats direction lock as a deliberate moment rather than an automatic outcome.
