# Signal Patterns

Signal patterns describe the recurring ways meaningful signals emerge during SWARM exploration.

Signals are not conclusions.

They are observations that begin to show structure across multiple loops.

A single observation rarely forms a signal. Signals strengthen when patterns repeat, converge, or intensify over time.

Signal patterns help practitioners recognize when something may deserve deeper attention.

---

# Recurrence

The same observation appears repeatedly across multiple loops.

The observation may appear in slightly different forms, but the underlying idea or friction continues to return.

Indicators:

- the observation appears in multiple artifacts
- the idea resurfaces after periods of exploration
- different exploration paths lead back to the same point

Recurrence is one of the most reliable indicators that a signal may be forming.

---

# Convergence

Multiple independent observations begin pointing toward the same direction.

No single observation is strong enough on its own, but together they suggest a consistent pattern.

Indicators:

- several weak observations cluster around the same theme
- different exploration paths produce similar insights
- observations from different sources point to the same direction

Convergence often strengthens weak signals.

---

# Amplification

An artifact produces stronger follow-up observations.

Creating something — a prototype, diagram, model, or experiment — reveals new observations that reinforce an emerging signal.

Indicators:

- new insights appear immediately after artifact creation
- artifacts expose deeper structure in the problem space
- producing work generates stronger feedback

Amplification is a key reason SWARM emphasizes artifact creation.

Artifacts generate new observations that clarify signals.

---

# Persistent Curiosity

An observation repeatedly attracts attention despite a lack of clear explanation.

The practitioner feels compelled to return to the idea across multiple loops.

Indicators:

- the idea resurfaces without deliberate effort
- the practitioner repeatedly returns to the same area of exploration
- curiosity remains strong even without immediate payoff

Persistent curiosity often indicates an early-stage signal.

---

# Emotional Charge

An observation carries unusual emotional weight.

This may appear as excitement, discomfort, frustration, or strong attraction to a direction.

Indicators:

- a reaction stronger than expected
- the observation feels unusually important
- the practitioner continues thinking about the observation outside active work

Emotional charge can indicate that a signal is forming, but it must be validated through repeated observations.

---

# Friction

The same obstacle appears repeatedly during exploration.

The friction may appear as confusion, inefficiency, or repeated failure points.

Indicators:

- the same difficulty appears across multiple loops
- attempts to bypass the obstacle fail
- artifacts repeatedly expose the same structural problem

Persistent friction often signals a deeper structural issue worth exploring.

---

# Unexpected Clarity

A previously confusing area suddenly resolves.

The practitioner experiences a moment where multiple observations align into a coherent structure.

Indicators:

- previously disconnected observations suddenly connect
- the structure of the problem becomes easier to describe
- direction becomes easier to articulate

Unexpected clarity often indicates that signals have reached sufficient strength to guide direction.

---

# Signal Strength

Signals strengthen when multiple signal patterns overlap.

For example:

- recurrence + convergence
- amplification + curiosity
- friction + convergence

The more patterns reinforce an observation, the stronger the signal becomes.

SWARM treats signals as hypotheses for attention, not conclusions.

Signals guide exploration but do not determine outcomes.
