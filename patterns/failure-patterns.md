# Failure Patterns

Failure patterns describe the recurring ways SWARM exploration can break down.

These patterns do not represent mistakes in individuals.  
They represent structural behaviors that appear when exploration loses balance.

Recognizing failure patterns helps practitioners restore healthy exploration and prevent protocol drift.

Failure patterns can appear at any stage of the SWARM loop.

---

# Noise Confusion

Random observations are mistaken for meaningful signals.

Indicators:

- conclusions form from isolated observations
- weak observations are treated as strong signals
- exploration direction changes rapidly without pattern reinforcement

Signals require repetition and convergence across loops.

Single observations rarely justify directional decisions.

---

# Novelty Bias

New ideas are treated as important simply because they are new.

Indicators:

- recent observations dominate attention
- older observations are ignored despite recurring presence
- exploration shifts constantly toward new stimuli

Novelty bias often prevents signals from strengthening across loops.

Healthy exploration allows ideas to persist long enough for patterns to emerge.

---

# AI Interpretation Error

AI-generated outputs are mistaken for meaning rather than treated as observations.

Indicators:

- AI responses are accepted without verification
- explanations generated by AI replace human interpretation
- AI output becomes the primary source of direction

Within SWARM, AI systems assist exploration but do not determine meaning.

Human practitioners remain responsible for interpreting signals.

---

# Over-Structuring

Structure is imposed before signals have strengthened.

Indicators:

- frameworks appear before sufficient observations exist
- organization becomes more important than exploration
- refinement attempts to finalize weak directions

Early structure can suppress emerging signals.

Exploration should remain flexible while signals are still forming.

---

# Artifact Avoidance

Loops continue without producing artifacts.

Indicators:

- exploration remains conceptual
- observations repeat without generating new material
- ideas circulate without being tested through creation

Artifacts create new observation surfaces.

Avoiding artifacts often prevents signals from strengthening.

---

# Exploration Addiction

Exploration continues indefinitely without directional commitment.

Indicators:

- loops expand continuously without narrowing
- refinement never eliminates directions
- artifacts remain exploratory rather than directional

Exploration addiction often occurs when practitioners avoid the risk of committing to a direction.

Healthy exploration eventually transitions toward decision checkpoints.

---

# Single-Signal Error

A direction is chosen based on a single signal.

Indicators:

- decisions occur after one strong observation
- supporting observations are weak or absent
- artifacts attempt to confirm the signal rather than test it

Reliable signals usually emerge through multiple reinforcing patterns.

Signals gain strength through recurrence, convergence, and amplification.

---

# Protocol Drift

The SWARM loop becomes partially or incorrectly applied.

Indicators:

- loop stages are skipped or collapsed
- observations are not captured during Spot
- artifacts are produced without refinement
- decisions occur without decision checkpoints

Protocol drift gradually reduces the effectiveness of SWARM.

Recognizing drift allows practitioners to restore the full loop.
