# Loop Logging

Loop logging is an applied SWARM practice for preserving carry-forward meaning from a loop.

It is not part of the SWARM protocol.

It is not required to run SWARM.

It is a portable practice that can help practitioners preserve signal, continuity, and judgment across repeated loops.

It is a repeatable post-loop preservation practice, not a separate framework.

---

## Origin

Loop logging emerged through Cardeo Root.

Cardeo Root was the first real operational expression of the practice. It did not merely document loop logging. It proved the need for it through use.

Historical lineage:

SWARM concepts -> Cardeo Root implementation -> Loop Log emergence -> SWARM practice formalization

Cardeo Root asks:

Does this deserve preservation?

SWARM Practice asks:

How can loop logging be used to preserve signal across loops?

The practice is abstracted here so it can travel beyond Cardeo Root.

---

## Relationship to SWARM

SWARM operates through repeated loops:

Spot -> Weigh -> Arrange -> Refine -> Make

Most loops do not produce durable outcomes.

Some loops produce observations, tensions, decisions, or patterns worth carrying forward.

Loop logging gives those moments a lightweight capture surface.

Some practices shape investigative loops.

Loop logging shapes preservation after a loop.

A loop log does not define SWARM.
It does not replace the protocol.
It does not make a loop complete.
It does not declare signal final.

It preserves material that may matter in later loops.

---

## Entry

Use loop logging when a loop has produced something worth preserving beyond the immediate activity.

Good entry conditions include:

- a shift in understanding
- a recurring pattern
- a useful tension
- a decision that affects future loops
- a signal candidate that needs to be watched
- an unexpected connection
- a repeated friction point
- a change in judgment
- an artifact that revealed new observations

Do not begin by assuming a log is needed.

First ask whether anything emerged that should be carried forward.

---

## Orientation

Before writing a loop log, distinguish between activity and signal.

Activity is what happened.

Signal is what may continue to matter.

A loop log should focus on the second.

Useful questions:

- What changed through this loop?
- What became clearer?
- What repeated?
- What should be remembered before the next loop?
- What remains uncertain?
- What might become useful across other contexts?
- What did the practitioner decide?
- What should not be over-interpreted yet?

The goal is not to summarize everything.

The goal is to preserve the smallest useful record of carry-forward meaning.

---

## Engagement

A loop log may include:

- context
- observations
- candidate signal
- evidence
- friction
- decisions
- open questions
- next loop direction
- carry-forward meaning

Keep the structure lightweight.

Move through the material as needed. Do not force every section if the loop does not require it.

A useful loop log should make the next loop more informed without making the practice feel bureaucratic.

---

## Relationship to the SWARM Loop

Loop logging can support each SWARM phase.

Spot
Preserve observations that may otherwise disappear.

Weigh
Name what appeared important, tense, repeated, or worth testing again.

Arrange
Group observations into a provisional structure.

Refine
Separate durable signal from noise, while preserving uncertainty.

Make
Produce a written artifact that can inform future loops.

The log itself is a Make artifact.

It may become input for later Spot, Weigh, Arrange, Refine, or Make phases.

---

## Signal Awareness

Loop logs are useful when signal may be forming but is not yet stable.

Signals are observed through repetition.

A loop log should preserve candidate signal without pretending it is final.

Useful signal indicators include:

- recurrence
- convergence
- amplification
- persistent curiosity
- emotional charge
- friction
- unexpected clarity

A single loop log may point toward signal.

It does not prove signal.

Signal becomes stronger only through continued loops and human judgment.

---

## Human Role

The human practitioner decides what matters.

The human validates whether a pattern is meaningful.

The human approves durable interpretation.

The human determines whether the log should influence future work.

Loop logging should strengthen judgment, not outsource it.

---

## AI Role

AI may assist with loop logging.

AI may:

- draft a proposed log
- summarize observations
- organize messy material
- compare against prior logs
- surface candidate signal
- identify repeated patterns
- preserve unresolved questions
- suggest what may be worth carrying forward

AI must not:

- declare signal final
- create certainty where the practitioner has not approved it
- decide what matters
- turn every work session into a log
- convert the practice into a checklist
- erase ambiguity too early

When signal is not stable, AI should say so.

Uncertainty is part of the record.

---

## When to Create a Loop Log

Create a loop log when:

- something changed the practitioner's understanding
- repeated work revealed a pattern
- friction exposed a deeper structure
- a decision changed future loops
- an artifact produced meaningful new observations
- a candidate signal needs to be watched
- a project situation revealed something reusable
- the loop produced carry-forward meaning

Loop logs are especially useful when work spans multiple sessions, tools, projects, or collaborators.

---

## When Not to Create a Loop Log

Do not create a loop log when:

- only routine progress occurred
- the material is only a task list
- the update is only project status
- nothing changed in understanding
- no reusable signal appeared
- the log would exist only to maintain a habit
- the insight is being forced
- the material belongs in a project note instead

Most loops do not need logs.

This is expected.

---

## What Loop Logs Are Not

Loop logs are not daily notes.

Daily notes record time and activity.

Loop logs preserve carry-forward meaning.

Loop logs are not daily task logs.

Daily task logs track execution over time.

Loop logs preserve signal, judgment, and memory that may matter in later loops.

Loop logs are not productivity logs.

Productivity logs track output, effort, or completion.

Loop logs track signal, judgment, and continuity.

Loop logs are not status updates.

Status updates communicate progress to others.

Loop logs preserve meaning for future loops.

Loop logs are not task lists.

Task lists organize execution.

Loop logs preserve interpretation.

Loop logs are not canon.

Canon contains stabilized concepts.

Loop logs may contain unstable or emerging material.

Loop logs are not protocol.

Protocol defines the governing structure of SWARM.

Loop logging is an optional applied practice.

---

## Anti-Patterns

### Logging Everything

If everything is logged, signal becomes harder to find.

Loop logging depends on selection.

### Forcing Insight

Not every loop produces meaning.

A weak or ordinary loop should be allowed to remain ordinary.

### Bureaucracy

If the practice becomes mechanical, it is no longer helping judgment.

Use the smallest structure that preserves the signal.

### Premature Certainty

A candidate signal should not be treated as stable too early.

Preserve uncertainty when the pattern has not survived repetition.

### Confusing Activity With Signal

A busy session may produce no durable insight.

A quiet session may produce a meaningful shift.

The amount of work is not the measure.

---

## Exit

A loop log is complete when it preserves enough meaning to help future loops.

A good exit may include:

- what changed
- what signal may be forming
- what remains uncertain
- what should be watched
- what the next loop should consider

Do not over-polish.

Do not over-explain.

Leave the log clear enough to be useful later.

---

## Core Principle

Loop logging preserves carry-forward meaning from a loop.

It helps practitioners remember what may matter without turning every loop into doctrine.
