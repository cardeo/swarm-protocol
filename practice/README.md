# Practices

Practices are ways of engaging the SWARM loop in real work.

They are not instructions.

They are not step-by-step methods.

They are entry points into exploration.

They shape movement and judgment, not predetermined outcomes.

---

## What a Practice Is

A practice is a repeatable way to enter or complete a SWARM loop.

One practice should map cleanly to one repeatable loop.

A practice should be:

- runnable
- completable
- repeatable
- capable of producing signal, learning, or next-loop pressure

Practices are not:

- playbooks
- checklists
- prompt packs
- domain taxonomies

If something requires many different loop shapes, it may be:

- a practice family
- a specialization
- an example
- a project
- an applied artifact

But it may not be a single practice yet.

Domain-specific files may exist when they clarify how a shared practice shape applies to a specific kind of system.

---

## How to Use a Practice

A practice is something you enter, not something you execute.

You may:

- move through sections nonlinearly
- repeat parts of the practice multiple times
- stop and restart as signals emerge

There is no requirement to complete a practice in a single pass.

---

## What to Focus On

Practices are designed to:

- surface signals
- sharpen judgment
- reveal what matters

The goal is not to “finish” the practice.

The goal is to notice what emerges while using it.

---

## What Practices Are Not

Practices are not:

- checklists
- rigid workflows
- guaranteed outcomes

If a practice feels mechanical, you are likely outside the loop.

---

## Relationship to the Protocol

Practices sit on top of the SWARM protocol.

They provide a way to apply the loop in specific contexts.

Use the protocol to understand how SWARM works.

Use practices to apply it.

Some practices may be grouped by domain or type.

These groupings do not create new protocol layers.

They simply make it easier to find relevant entry points for particular kinds of work.

---

## Evolving Practices

Practices may change over time.

As new signals emerge, practices can be refined, expanded, or replaced.

They are part of the system, not separate from it.
