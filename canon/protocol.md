# Protocol

## Definition

A protocol is the pattern that emerges across repeated loops once coherence proves durable.

Protocols are not designed in advance.  
They are discovered through repetition.

As loops accumulate, certain structures begin to repeat.  
Certain constraints repeatedly matter.  
Certain decisions consistently strengthen signal.

When those patterns become clear enough to recognize, the protocol has emerged.

Protocol is not the starting point of work.  
It is the structure revealed through sustained looping.

## Protocol Is Revealed in Hindsight

In SWARM, protocol is rarely visible at the beginning.

Early loops can feel like repetition without structure.  
Directions appear, collapse, and reform.

Over time, patterns begin to surface.

You may find yourself repeatedly returning to the same constraint.  
You may notice that certain reductions consistently strengthen clarity.  
You may observe that similar loop structures continue producing signal.

Only after enough loops does the connecting pattern become visible.

Protocol is recognized after the fact.

It is the structure that explains why certain loops worked.

Protocol is not declared at the start.  
It is named once the pattern becomes undeniable. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## Protocol as Memory

Protocol preserves the learning generated across loops.

Without protocol, each loop would operate in isolation.

Protocol captures recurring patterns such as:

- recurring constraints
- recurring structures
- recurring evaluation behaviors
- recurring reductions

This accumulated memory strengthens future loops.

Each new loop begins from a more informed position.

---

## Protocol and Reduction

SWARM advances through reduction rather than expansion.

Across repeated loops, unnecessary complexity collapses.

What remains is the minimal structure required to consistently produce signal.

Protocol represents this reduced structure.

It is not a comprehensive framework.

It is the smallest repeatable pattern that continues to generate signal.

---

## Human Judgment and Protocol

Protocol supports judgment.

It does not replace it.

AI systems may assist in generating variation or organizing information, but protocol recognition requires human evaluation across many loops.

Agents may assist with:

- running loops
- generating variations
- organizing results
- identifying possible patterns

Agents must not declare protocol formation.

Protocol recognition depends on human judgment and accumulated loop experience.

---

## Protocol Drift

Protocols can degrade if misapplied.

Common failure modes include:

- imposing protocol before sufficient loops exist
- mistaking temporary patterns for durable ones
- converting protocol into rigid workflow systems
- optimizing protocol for speed rather than signal

Protocol should remain adaptable as new loops continue to generate information.

When signals shift, protocols must evolve.

---

## Protocol Stability

A protocol becomes stable when:

- repeated loops consistently produce signal
- similar reductions repeatedly strengthen clarity
- similar evaluation patterns guide future loops

At that point, the protocol becomes a reliable structure for continued exploration.

However, protocol stability should never prevent new loops.

SWARM strengthens through continued movement.
