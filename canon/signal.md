# Signal

## Definition

A signal is a pattern that persists across repeated loops.

Signals are not declared.  
They are observed through repetition.

When an idea, constraint, or structure continues to appear across multiple loops, it begins to accumulate weight.

Weak directions collapse under repetition.  
Strong signals persist.

Signal represents meaning that survives iteration.

SWARM operates through three interacting elements.

Loop produces movement.  
Protocol preserves learning across loops.  
Signal represents what persists.

Loop = movement

Signals do not appear within a single loop.  
They emerge across many loops.

## How Signals Form

Signals form through repetition.

Each loop produces information.

Most directions disappear quickly.  
Some directions begin to repeat.

When a pattern continues to survive evaluation across loops, it gains strength.

Signals strengthen when they:

- appear repeatedly
- survive reduction
- continue producing useful outcomes

Signals weaken when they:

- fail under repetition
- collapse when constraints change
- disappear after evaluation

Signal strength increases through repeated validation.

---

## Signal and Reduction

Reduction is the mechanism that clarifies signal.

As loops repeat, unnecessary elements collapse.

What remains is the minimal structure that continues to work.

Reduction removes noise.  
Signal survives reduction.

If an idea disappears when simplified, it was not signal.

If it persists under reduction, it gains weight.

---

## Signal and Human Judgment

Signal detection requires judgment.

AI systems can generate variation and assist with exploration.

However, signal recognition depends on human evaluation.

The human determines:

- which outcomes matter
- which patterns persist
- which directions deserve another loop

Agents may surface potential patterns, but they must not declare signal.

Signal detection remains a human responsibility.

---

## Signal vs Trend

Signals are not trends.

Trends are external patterns driven by popularity or timing.

Signals are internal patterns that persist through repeated loops.

A trend may appear briefly and disappear.

A signal continues to strengthen through use.

Signals are discovered through practice, not observation alone.

---

## Signal Density

Signal becomes visible when loops accumulate.

A single loop rarely produces signal.

Across many loops, patterns become easier to recognize.

Higher loop density produces:

- clearer signals
- stronger judgment
- faster recognition of weak directions

Signal detection improves as loop experience increases.

---

## False Signals

False signals appear when evaluation happens too early.

Common causes include:

- declaring signal after too few loops
- mistaking novelty for persistence
- confusing external validation with internal strength
- bundling too many variables into a single loop

Running more loops clarifies whether a signal is real.

Weak signals collapse under repetition.

Strong signals survive it.

---

## Signals and Protocol Formation

Signals are the foundation of protocol.

When signals persist across loops, the structures that support them begin to repeat.

Those repeating structures form protocol.

Protocol emerges when signal becomes consistent.

Signals create meaning.  
Protocol organizes that meaning.

---

## Stability

Signals become stable when they consistently survive:

- repetition
- reduction
- evaluation

Stable signals guide future loops.

However, signals must remain open to reevaluation.

New loops may strengthen them, refine them, or replace them.

SWARM remains adaptive through continued looping.
