# SWARM Loop

The SWARM protocol operates through a five-stage loop.

Spot → Weigh → Arrange → Refine → Make

Each pass through these stages is called a **loop**.

Loops are the basic unit of movement within SWARM.  
Work progresses by repeating the loop rather than executing a fixed sequence of steps once.

Most loops do not produce finished outcomes.

They generate momentum, reveal structure, and allow signals to emerge over time.

---

# Loop Stages

## Spot

Capture observations without evaluating them.

Observations may include ideas, friction points, behaviors, anomalies, or questions.

The goal of Spot is expansion — widening the field of possible inputs.

Early filtering is avoided to prevent premature conclusions.

---

## Weigh

Evaluate the observations collected during Spot.

Some observations may begin to look more important than others.

Practitioners consider:

- repetition
- connections between observations
- potential impact on the problem being explored

Weak observations often collapse at this stage.

Observations that persist begin to gain weight.

---

## Arrange

Organize observations into meaningful structure.

Common arrangements include:

- clusters
- patterns
- workflows
- systems
- sequences of events

Multiple promising structures may emerge.

The purpose of Arrange is to reveal relationships between observations and surface possible directions for exploration.

---

## Refine

Clarify which direction should be pursued.

When several strong arrangements exist, the practitioner selects one to focus on.

Other promising directions may be parked for later loops.

Refinement focuses attention while preserving the broader exploration.

---

## Make

Produce a concrete output that advances the chosen direction.

The purpose of Make is momentum.

Outputs may include sketches, prototypes, experiments, decisions, or other small artifacts that move the work forward.

These outputs often generate new observations, which feed the next loop.

---

# Loop Behavior

The SWARM loop is not linear.

Each pass through the loop produces new observations, which feed back into the next cycle.

Over time, patterns begin to appear across loops.

When these patterns persist, they may become **signals**.

Signals indicate that meaningful structure is emerging from repeated exploration.
