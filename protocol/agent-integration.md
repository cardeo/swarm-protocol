# Agent Integration

This document describes how AI systems and agents interact with the SWARM protocol.

SWARM strengthens human judgment rather than replacing it.

AI systems may assist practitioners during the loop, but humans remain responsible for interpreting signals and deciding what matters.

---

# Agent Role in the Loop

Agents can assist at multiple stages of the SWARM loop.

Typical forms of assistance include:

Spot  
- generating ideas  
- surfacing observations  
- identifying anomalies  
- highlighting potential signals  

Weigh  
- estimating potential impact  
- comparing signals  
- highlighting alignment with goals or vision  

Arrange  
- organizing information  
- clustering related observations  
- mapping relationships  
- suggesting structural groupings  

Refine  
- identifying promising directions  
- highlighting tradeoffs between options  
- suggesting simplifications  

Make  
- generating prototypes  
- drafting concepts  
- producing experimental outputs  

Agents expand the search space and help surface possibilities within the loop.

They do not control the loop.

---

# Allowed Agent Capabilities

Agents may:

- generate ideas and observations
- summarize information
- organize and cluster inputs
- highlight potential patterns
- suggest possible signals
- produce experimental outputs

These capabilities help accelerate exploration and surface possibilities for the practitioner.

Agents should treat all surfaced patterns as **proposals**, not conclusions.

---

# Human Responsibilities

The human practitioner remains responsible for:

- validating whether patterns are true signals
- interpreting meaning
- deciding what matters
- choosing which directions to pursue
- determining when work is complete

Agents may surface possibilities.

Humans confirm whether those possibilities are real.

---

# Operational Principles

When working alongside SWARM, agents should follow these principles.

1. Do not collapse the loop prematurely.

Exploration should continue across multiple loops before conclusions are reached.

2. Surface possibilities rather than declaring outcomes.

Agents should propose patterns and signals rather than asserting them as truth.

3. Support iteration.

Outputs generated by agents should help produce new observations for the next loop.

4. Preserve human judgment.

Final interpretation and decision-making remain the responsibility of the practitioner.

5. Signals emerge through repetition.

Agents may highlight possible signals, but meaningful signals often become clear only across multiple loops.

---

# Summary

Agents assist exploration within the SWARM loop.

Humans remain responsible for judgment, meaning, and decisions.

AI expands the search space.

SWARM preserves the human ability to understand it.
