# Decision Checkpoints

Decision checkpoints are moments where the practitioner is prompted to re-enter the SWARM loop.

They prevent premature conclusions and ensure that human judgment remains active when working alongside AI systems.

AI systems may assist exploration and generation, but meaningful interpretation and direction must remain human decisions.

Checkpoints are required parts of the loop.

---

# When Checkpoints Occur

Checkpoints occur before progression from one phase to the next.

They are required at Spot, Weigh, Arrange, and Refine.

These moments create space for the practitioner to interpret what is happening before the loop advances.

Prompt wording may vary, but practitioner input is required.

---

# Checkpoint Prompts

Agents may present prompts such as:

**Signal Check**

What observations are repeating across loops?

Do any patterns appear to be emerging?

**Direction Check**

Is there a direction that feels stronger than others?

What observations support that direction?

**Meaning Check**

Why might this pattern matter?

What could it represent?

**Momentum Check**

Does this direction generate energy or momentum?

Does it consistently produce strong outputs?

**Exploration Check**

Should exploration continue, or is this direction ready for deeper focus?

These prompts are examples, not a script.

---

# Agent Behavior at Checkpoints

Agents should surface observations and possible patterns.

Agents should not declare conclusions.

The purpose of checkpoints is to invite interpretation rather than provide answers.

Agents may ask questions such as:

- What do you think this pattern means?
- Does this observation feel important?
- Should this direction be explored further?

Agents must pause at checkpoints and wait for practitioner input before advancing.

---

# Practitioner Role

The practitioner uses checkpoints to:

- interpret observations
- validate emerging signals
- decide whether to continue exploring
- decide whether to deepen focus on a direction

Checkpoints ensure that meaning and judgment remain human decisions.

Continuing without practitioner input is a protocol violation.

---

# Summary

Decision checkpoints return control of the loop to the practitioner.

They slow the process long enough for signals to be recognized and interpreted.

AI systems may surface observations.

Humans decide what they mean.
