# Signals Protocol

This document describes how signals emerge within the SWARM protocol.

Signals are not the starting point of the loop.

The loop operates on observations.  
Signals emerge when patterns become visible across multiple loops.

For the philosophical definition of signals, see:

../canon/signal.md

---

# Observations vs Signals

During a SWARM loop, practitioners work with **observations**.

Observations include:

- notes
- ideas
- sketches
- artifacts
- reactions
- experiments
- questions
- outputs from AI systems

Observations are raw material.

Signals appear later, when patterns begin to repeat or strengthen across loops.

---

# What Signals Are

A signal is an indication that something meaningful may be emerging.

Signals appear when observations repeatedly point in the same direction.

They are not conclusions or final answers.

Signals indicate that attention may be worth focusing on a particular area.

Signals often become visible only after multiple loops of exploration.

---

# What Signals Can Look Like

Signals can emerge in many forms, including:

- a concept repeatedly producing strong results
- an idea that keeps resurfacing
- unexpected quality appearing in outputs
- a design direction gaining clarity
- a pattern appearing across different experiments
- sustained curiosity around a particular area
- momentum building around a specific approach

Signals are often **positive indicators of direction**, not just problems.

They may represent opportunity, resonance, or emerging strength.

---

# Signal Strength

Signals become clearer through repetition.

Weak signals may appear occasionally.

Stronger signals often:

- appear across multiple loops
- appear in different contexts
- consistently produce high-quality outputs
- generate excitement or momentum
- align with other emerging signals

Practitioners should allow signals to develop naturally through continued looping.

---

# Signals and the SWARM Loop

The SWARM loop works on observations.

Observations move through the stages:

Spot  
Capture observations.

Weigh  
Evaluate which observations appear meaningful.

Arrange  
Organize observations into structures or patterns.

Refine  
Test and strengthen promising directions.

Make  
Produce artifacts and experiments.

Each loop produces new observations.

Signals emerge when patterns become visible across multiple loops.

---

# Signals and Agents

AI systems can assist by working with observations.

Examples include:

- generating ideas
- analyzing outputs
- organizing information
- surfacing patterns
- highlighting repeated themes

Agents may suggest potential signals, but they cannot determine whether a signal is meaningful.

Signals become real only when a practitioner recognizes a pattern emerging through repeated loops.

Agents expand the search space.

Humans recognize the signal.

---

# Summary

The SWARM loop operates on observations.

Signals are patterns that emerge from repeated loops.

They indicate that something meaningful may be forming.

Signals guide attention and direction, but they do not represent final conclusions.
